Local Friends Caught Reading Each Other’s Minds

Experts baffled as a single clue leads an entire group to the very same dot

By our Picture Desk, from the front row · Doors at 8:02 p.m.

Ladies, gentlemen and undecided parties: each round, one brave performer, the “psychic”, is shown a secret point on a board marked with two pairs of opposites. Funny and Unfunny. Ugly and Cute.

They may offer the audience a single clue. Not two. One. The audience then marks, in secret, where they believe the point lies, and the closest guesses earn the most points.

“It took thirty seconds to learn,” said one player, “and three hours to stop arguing about whether a goose is scary.” No downloads or accounts are required.

Our photographers have been sent to every show in town. They report the same thing each time: one clue, one dot, and a room full of people pointing at it.
