Step Right Up and Read Your Friends’ Minds

Programme notes · Doors at 8:02 p.m.

Ladies, gentlemen and undecided parties: each round, one brave performer, the “psychic”, is shown a secret point on a board marked with two pairs of opposites. Funny and Unfunny. Ugly and Cute.

They may offer the audience a single clue. Not two. One. The audience then marks, in secret, where they believe the point lies, and the closest guesses earn the most points.

Witnesses have described the act as “thirty seconds to learn and three hours to stop arguing about whether a goose is scary.”

Rules of playHow the Game Is Played

I. The Psychic Takes a Peek

One player privately sees where the target sits between two pairs of opposing words. Nobody else may look.

II. A Single Clue Is Given

Something that belongs at that exact spot. A pug in a hot dog costume, for instance: rather cute, and somewhat funny.

III. Readers Place Their Marks

Every other player marks the board in secret. Then all is revealed. A bullseye earns four points; the highest total wins.
