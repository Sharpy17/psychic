Local Friends Caught Reading Each Other’s Minds

Experts baffled as a single clue leads an entire group to the very same dot

Staff photo. (Picture desk)

By our Picture Desk · Developed 8:02 p.m. · Reload for a new front page

A new party game is sweeping living rooms and video calls alike. Each round, one player, the “psychic”, is shown a secret point on a board marked with two pairs of opposites, such as Funny and Unfunny, or Ugly and Cute.

The psychic may offer just one clue. The remaining players then mark, in secret, where they believe the point lies. When the target is revealed, the closest guesses earn the most points.

“It took thirty seconds to learn,” said one player, “and three hours to stop arguing about whether a goose is scary.” No downloads or accounts are required.

Our photographers have been sent to every game night in town. They report the same thing each time: one clue, one dot, and a room full of people pointing at it.
